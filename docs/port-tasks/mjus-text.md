# Project Justice text sources

Written 2026-10-19. What is read, and why the rest is not yet.

## Read: the immediate ASCII draw

- `0x140B96130` draws a string at once: ecx x, edx y, xmm2 scale, r9d
  colour, the string on the stack. Bytes above 0x10 draw glyph table
  0x141792D20[byte]; others only move the pen.
- Seven callers: the credit task (routine 1, 0x140BA0D00) with INSERT %d MORE
  COIN(S), CREDIT(S) and a coin fraction; the copyright line; ROUND BONUS and
  its score (0x140BC9439).
- `src/mjus_text.c` hooks it, collects each update's strings and publishes
  them after the update returns, through the shared reader
  (`src/game_text.c`). The credit display is never narrated, as for every
  game; the shared filter now also knows "CREDIT(S) 3" and "1/2". The `` ` ``
  key reads it all.
- Test: `.\tools\run_mjus_text_test.ps1`.

## Not readable as text: story dialogue, names and menus (history)

- The only strings Project Justice's native code references are those above,
  plus EDIT SELECT and NO DATA (0x140C34B2E, 0x140C34DE7, another draw path,
  not hooked yet).
- The ROM blob (`bin/mjus` in game_60.arc) holds no English as ASCII or
  UTF-16; its AFS entries are compressed.
- Guest main RAM (`*(*0x142C79B40 + 8)`, guest 0x0C000000), saved every 4 s
  through the story scenes by `tools/guest_ram_snap.py`: no English text, and
  no character name under any constant byte or 16-bit offset.
- So the dialogue is drawn as pictures. Story scenes are bytecode
  (R+0x2230 points into scripts such as 0x1417B35D0), run by task routine 7
  (0x140BA16D0) through 0x140BCB930, 0x140BCBAD0 and 0x140BA5440.

## Story dialogue and narration: spoken (2026-10-20)

Built from the pictures, since there is no text. `src/mjus_story.c`,
table `src/mjus_story_text.h` generated by `tools/make_mjus_story.py` from
`data/mjus_story/`. Not yet heard in-game.

### The pictures

- Dialogue: AFS entry 34 (Japanese) and 35 (English) of game_60.arc, chosen
  by 0x140BA7F20. 100 pieces, one per scene ID; each piece is a run of
  0x8000-byte cells, 128x128 twiddled ARGB4444, one message per cell. A
  message reads as rows 0-55 then rows 56-111 placed to their right (the
  screen draws the cell's two halves as two quads side by side).
- Narration: entry 26 (Japanese) and 27 (English), chosen by 0x140BA7A70.
  30 pieces of 256x256; a screen is two pieces side by side, so 15 screens,
  P00..P14.
- Language: 0x140B9B7E0 returns 1 when R+0x206C is 0, and 1 selects entry 34
  and 26. So R+0x206C = 0 means Japanese. Both story captures had 1.

### Transcription

- `data/mjus_story/en.tsv` (1054 cells) and `ja.tsv` (1065 cells), one line
  per cell, keyed SS.CC (scene, cell). `#WORDS` marks a scene's words cell,
  `#GLYPH` a lone icon; neither is ever shown on its own.
- `{}` marks a blank the game fills at run time. The fills are in
  `fills.tsv`, and each was checked against the piece the game overlays
  (below), not guessed. 24 English and 22 Japanese messages have them.
- `nar_en.tsv`, `nar_ja.tsv`: the 15 narration screens, one line each.
- `notes.txt`: the three Japanese readings that are uncertain (blurred
  12-pixel glyphs), and the one doubled English "the".

### Word overlays

- The per-scene struct (tables 0x1417B2F20 Japanese, 0x1417B3240 English,
  indexed by scene; stride 0x28) holds +0x00 script, +0x08 message count,
  +0x18 a height byte per message, +0x20 a pointer per message to a list of
  pieces. Each piece is seven int16: words cell, source x, y, width, height,
  destination x, y; a negative first word ends the list. Drawn by the loop at
  0x140C37940.
- Source coordinates are in half pixels of the 128-pixel cell (the UV scale
  at 0x14106E680 is 1/256). With the right table-to-pack pairing, the pieces
  land on whole words; with the wrong pairing they cut words in half, which
  is how the pairing was settled.
- 20x20 pieces are Momo's heart icons. Not spoken.

### What is on screen

- R+0x2259 scene ID. Two message records at R+0x2600 + side*0x5C, one per
  speaker side: +0x24 1 shown, 2 closing, 0 none; +0x2C the message, which
  is the cell index. Written by the scene interpreter 0x140C367E0 (opcode 5
  via 0x140C36784; opcode 6 sets closing).
- A side turning to shown with a new (scene, message) is one new line. A
  closing record keeps its line, because one speaker's record stays closing
  while the other speaks. An emptied record re-arms, so a replayed scene is
  read again.
- Story mode is task routine 7 (0x140BA16D0). Its record's +7 is the part:
  1 intro (0x140BCB930), 2 dialogue scenes (0x140BCBAD0), 3 the rest
  (0x140BA5440); +8 the step. The intro loads screen 0 for schools 0..5,
  then screens 2*school+1 and 2*school+2 (school R+0x2238); Darkside (6)
  gets 13 and 14 only.

### Speech

- The MJUS frame hook calls `mjus_story_frame()` after each update. New
  dialogue lines go to one queue; on entering the intro, that school's
  screens go to a separate narration queue. The worker speaks narration
  first, then dialogue, one line per tick, advancing only on acceptance.
- When the intro ends with narration still recent (90 s), unspoken screens
  are dropped and speech is stopped, so skipping the intro is not talked
  over by its own narration.
- Wordless pictures ("......") are pauses and are not spoken.
- The `` ` `` key reads the narration screens during the intro, otherwise the
  message(s) on screen; outside story scenes it defers to the general
  Project Justice source.

### Tests

- `.\tools\run_mjus_story_test.ps1`: regenerates nothing but fails if the
  table is stale; replays two live story scenes
  (`tools/fixtures/mjus-story-20261019.bin`, built by
  `tools/make_mjus_story_fixture.py` from captures mjus-1 and mjus-3) and
  checks the line order, both intros and the page lists, lookups, the
  language flag, retry and re-arming. The test places fixture bytes at the
  game's addresses literally, so a wrong offset in the header cannot cancel
  itself out (the sabotage sweep caught that once).
- `python tools\sabotage_mjus.py`: 15 story sabotages among the rest.
